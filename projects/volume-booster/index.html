Volume Booster

Volume Booster by Derek Roga is his second product. It follows the same rule as TestPrintPage: one job, done carefully, nothing bolted on.

What is verified

Volume Booster is Derek's second product. That is the honest summary of what is public about it today: it exists, it is his, and it is built on the same rule as TestPrintPage. One job, done carefully, nothing bolted on.

There is no feature list on this page, no download link, no price and no launch date, because none of those have been published yet. When they are, this page will carry them. Until then, anything more specific would be guessing, and this site does not guess about Derek's work.

The one-job idea

"One job, done carefully, nothing bolted on" is a design rule, not a slogan. It means the product does one thing, and everything in it serves that one thing. No settings panel full of options nobody asked for. No second feature bolted on because a competitor has one. No account, no onboarding tour, no upsell screen standing between you and the job you came to do.

TestPrintPage is the worked example. It prints test pages. It does not manage your printer, sell you ink, or track your printing history. The five sheets each answer one question, and the site gets out of the way once you have the PDF. Volume Booster is the same rule applied to a second product: one job, done carefully.

The rule costs something. A one-job tool will always look thin next to a suite that does twelve things badly. It is harder to describe, because there is no long feature list to lean on. And it demands discipline, because every new idea has to survive the question "does this serve the one job, or is it bolted on?" But the saving is real too: a small tool stays maintainable by one person, stays fast, and stays understandable. The full essay on the idea is Building Small Tools.

Volume help

General guides, with no product claims attached.

The sibling product

The first product built on this rule is a free printer test page utility with five symptom-matched sheets, launched in November 2026. If you want to see what "one job, done carefully" looks like in practice, that is the place to look.
