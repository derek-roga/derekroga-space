Streaks and Lines on Prints

Lines, streaks or bands across the page. Read the pattern first: horizontal or vertical, regular or random, colour or black only. The pattern is the diagnosis.

Read the pattern

Before touching anything, look at the print and answer three questions. The answers narrow the cause ladder to one or two suspects.

QuestionIf yesPoints at
Are the lines horizontal, across the direction of travel?Lines run across the page, parallel to the top edgeClogged nozzles, or paper-feed skipping
Are the lines vertical, along the direction of travel?Lines run down the page, parallel to the side edgeDirty roller, or drum or transfer issue on a laser printer
Are the lines regular, evenly spaced?The same spacing repeats down the pageA roller or drum with a fixed circumference: the spacing is the roller's size
Are the lines random?No repeating patternClogged nozzles, or debris in the paper path
Are the lines in one colour only?Only cyan, only magenta, only yellow, or only blackThat channel's nozzles or cartridge
Are the lines in every colour?All colours show the same linesA shared fault: feed, roller, or drum

A useful trick for regular lines: measure the distance between repeats. If the lines repeat every 75mm, the fault is a roller with a 75mm circumference. Your printer's manual may list the circumferences of its rollers and drum, which turns a measurement into a named part.

The cause ladder

Clogged nozzles

The commonest cause of horizontal lines on an inkjet. Nozzles clog when the printer sits unused, and a clogged nozzle leaves a white line where it should have printed. The fix is one head-cleaning cycle from the printer's maintenance menu, then a reprint. Print the CMYK sheet to see which channel is affected: the banded bar names the clogged channel.

Dirty rollers

Rollers pick up paper dust, ink and debris, and a dirty roller leaves a repeating mark down the page. The fix: clean the rollers with a soft, lint-free cloth, lightly dampened with water, following your printer's manual. Never use solvents or alcohol unless the manual says to. If the marks repeat at a fixed interval, the roller is the prime suspect.

Drum or transfer issue, laser printers only

On a laser printer, the drum and the transfer roller are the parts that move toner onto the paper. A scratched or worn drum leaves vertical lines in the same place on every page. A damaged transfer roller leaves marks that repeat at the roller's circumference. These are laser-specific faults: an inkjet has no drum. The fix for a worn drum is a replacement drum unit, which on many laser printers is a separate part from the toner cartridge. Check your printer's manual for whether the drum is a user-replaceable part on your model.

Misalignment

A misaligned print head can leave lines where the head's passes do not meet cleanly, usually horizontal bands at the boundary between passes. The fix is the alignment utility: print the alignment sheet, run the utility, and reprint. If the lines clear after alignment, the head was the fault.

Low toner

On a laser printer, low toner gives pale vertical bands, often in the same place on every page, because the toner is not distributing evenly. The fix: take the toner cartridge out, rock it gently side to side to redistribute the remaining toner, and reprint. If the bands clear, the cartridge is low and a replacement is due. If they do not clear, the fault is elsewhere in the ladder.

Fix per cause, in order

  1. Read the pattern. Horizontal or vertical, regular or random, one colour or all. Write it down.
  2. If the lines are horizontal and random: run one head-cleaning cycle, then reprint.
  3. If the lines are vertical and regular: clean the rollers, and on a laser printer, check the drum.
  4. If the lines are horizontal and regular: check alignment, then the paper feed.
  5. Prove the fix: print the Alignment sheet, then the Black and White sheet. Both should be clean.

Do not clean what is not dirty. Head-cleaning cycles use ink, roller cleaning risks damage if done roughly, and drum units are expensive. The pattern reading exists so you clean the right part once, instead of cleaning everything three times. If you are not sure which part the pattern points at, print the Alignment and B&W sheets first: they narrow it down before you open the printer.

Prevention

Most streaks are preventable, and the prevention is undramatic. Print something at least once a week, even one page, to keep inkjet nozzles from drying out. A printer that sits idle for a month is a printer with clogged nozzles waiting to happen, and the first print after the idle month is the one that shows it. Keep the paper path clean: a quick wipe of the accessible rollers with a dry, lint-free cloth every few months removes the paper dust that causes repeating marks. Store paper flat, in its wrapper, in a dry room, because damp paper sheds more dust and feeds less cleanly.

On a laser printer, the drum is the part to protect. Never touch the drum surface, and never expose it to bright light for long: both damage the coating and leave permanent marks. When you change a toner cartridge, check the drum for marks or scratches while the printer is open, and replace the drum unit when the manual says its life is up, not when the lines become unbearable. A drum replaced on schedule never produces a streak. A drum replaced late has already ruined a ream of paper.
