Black and White Test Page

A black and white test page checks mono printing: grey steps, solid blacks and fine text. It is the sheet that catches banding, grey instead of black, and fuzzy text.

What it checks

Most printing is black and white: letters, forms, invoices, schoolwork, boarding passes. A black and white test page checks the parts of the printer that do that work. It has three elements, and each one tests something different.

  • Grey steps. A ramp from white to black in even increments. Each step should be visibly different from the next, and the darkest step should be a true black, not a dark grey.
  • Solid blacks. Large blocks of pure black. These show banding, streaking and uneven coverage that fine text hides.
  • Fine text. Small text at several sizes, which tests sharpness. Text is the hardest thing for a printer to get right at small sizes, so it is the most sensitive sharpness test on the sheet.

Print this sheet when black text looks grey, when large black areas look patchy, when text looks fuzzy, or when you want to prove a fix for faded prints. It is also the right sheet after a head cleaning or a toner change, because it shows immediately whether the black channel is healthy.

How to print one

Print the Black and White sheet from TestPrintPage, free, no sign-up. Pick the paper size that matches the paper in your tray, A4 or US Letter, and print at 100 percent with no scaling. Print in black and white mode if your driver offers it, so the sheet tests the black channel alone rather than a composite black made from colour inks. If you are not sure about the print dialog, the how to print a test page guide walks through it.

How to read it

The grey ramp

Look at the ramp from white to black. Each step should be distinct. If the lightest steps all look white, the printer is losing highlight detail, which usually means a quality setting is too low or the black cartridge is nearly empty. If the darkest steps all look the same black, the printer is crushing shadow detail, which can be a driver setting or a paper problem. If the ramp has a colour tint, pink, green or blue, the printer is mixing colour ink into the black, which means the black channel is weak and the driver is compensating.

The solid blacks

Hold the sheet at arm's length and look at the large black blocks. They should be one flat, even black. Horizontal bands across a black block mean the print head is skipping rows, usually a clogged nozzle or a feed problem. Vertical bands mean a dirty roller or a drum issue on a laser printer. Pale patches mean the toner or ink is not reaching the paper evenly. If the black looks grey rather than black, check the quality setting first: draft or economy mode prints grey by design.

The fine text

Read the smallest text on the sheet. The edges of the letters should be crisp. Fuzzy or soft edges at small sizes mean the head is slightly out of alignment, the paper is absorbing too much ink, or the print quality setting is too low. Doubled letters, where each letter has a faint shadow beside it, are an alignment fault: run the printer alignment test and your printer's alignment utility.

If the sheet shows faded output overall, the guide to faded prints has the full cause ladder. If text is doubled or offset, the alignment guide is the next step. In both cases, print this sheet again after the fix. The second print is the proof.

When to print this sheet

Print the Black and White sheet whenever black output is the question. That covers more situations than most people expect. Print it before an important document, a CV, a contract, a form that has to be legible, to confirm the black channel is healthy before the print that matters. Print it after a cartridge change, to confirm the new cartridge is printing properly and the head is clear. Print it after a head cleaning, as the proof that the cleaning worked. And print it when a document looks wrong but you cannot name what is wrong: the grey ramp and the solid blacks will name it for you.

It is also worth printing on a schedule, not just when something breaks. A printer that sits unused for weeks can develop a clogged head silently, and the first you hear of it is a faded document at the worst moment. One Black and White sheet a month, printed and glanced at, catches a clog while it is still a one-cycle cleaning job instead of a dried-out head. The sheet costs one page and thirty seconds. The document it saves may be the one you cannot reprint.
