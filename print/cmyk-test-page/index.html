CMYK Test Page

A CMYK test page isolates each ink channel, cyan, magenta, yellow and black, so you can name the failing channel instead of guessing at it.

What CMYK means

CMYK is the four-ink model almost every colour printer uses. C is cyan, a greenish blue. M is magenta, a purplish red. Y is yellow. K is black, called "key" because it provides the detail and depth the other three cannot. Every colour your printer makes is a mixture of these four inks, laid down in tiny dots. A red is mostly magenta plus yellow. A green is cyan plus yellow. A deep blue is cyan plus magenta.

That mixing is why colour faults are confusing. When a photo prints with a green tint, the fault is not "green ink", because there is no green ink. The fault is one of the four channels: too much cyan and yellow, or too little magenta. The CMYK sheet removes the confusion by printing each channel on its own, so you can see each one separately.

How the sheet isolates each channel

The CMYK sheet has four bars, one per channel, each printed in pure cyan, pure magenta, pure yellow or pure black. Each bar steps from light to full strength, so you can see not just whether the channel prints, but how well it prints across its range. A healthy channel shows a smooth bar from pale to full. A failing channel shows gaps, bands, or nothing at all.

Because each bar uses only one ink, there is no mixing to hide behind. If the cyan bar is blank, the cyan channel is not printing. If the magenta bar is banded, the magenta nozzles are partly clogged. If the yellow bar is pale, the yellow cartridge is low. The sheet turns a vague symptom, "the colours look wrong", into a specific fact: "the cyan channel is failing."

How to print one

Print the CMYK sheet from TestPrintPage, free, no sign-up. Pick the paper size that matches the paper in your tray, A4 or US Letter, and print at 100 percent with no scaling. Print in colour mode, not black and white, so all four channels are used. Let the print dry for a minute before judging it.

How to read it

Check each bar in turn, and write down what you see. The pattern across the four bars is the diagnosis.

What the bar showsWhat it meansWhat to do
Bar is blankChannel not printing: empty cartridge, or fully clogged headCheck the cartridge level, then run a head cleaning
Bar is banded or streakedSome nozzles in that channel are cloggedRun one head-cleaning cycle, then reprint
Bar is pale but evenCartridge low, or a quality setting is reducing inkCheck the level and the quality setting
Bar is fine, but colours are still wrongThe fault is not the ink: colour management or paper profileSee the wrong colours guide
All four bars are badA fault shared by every channel: head, feed, or paperClean the head, check the paper, check the feed

One practical note: run a head cleaning once, then reprint the sheet. If the bar improves but is not clean, run it a second time. Beyond two cycles, further cleaning wastes ink without helping, and the cartridge itself becomes the more likely fault. If the bar does not improve at all after two cleanings, check the cartridge, and consider that a refilled or third-party cartridge may be failing even while it reports ink remaining.

When it is not an ink problem

Sometimes all four bars print perfectly and the colours are still wrong. That is useful information: it rules out the ink and the heads, and points at colour management. The usual suspects are the paper type setting in the print dialog, which changes how much ink the driver lays down; double colour management, where both the application and the driver try to correct colour at once; and the wrong paper profile, which tells the driver the paper is something it is not. The printer printing wrong colours guide walks through each one.

The CMYK sheet is also the proof sheet after a fix. Print it before you change a cartridge or clean a head, and again afterwards. If the failing bar is healthy on the second print, the fix worked. If it is not, the fault is somewhere else, and you have not wasted a second cartridge finding out.

Composite black and the K channel

One thing the CMYK sheet reveals is whether your printer is making black from the black cartridge or from a mixture of colours. When the black channel is weak, many drivers quietly compensate by mixing cyan, magenta and yellow to make a composite black. The result looks black at a glance, but it is softer, slightly tinted, and it uses three cartridges to do one cartridge's job. The K bar on the CMYK sheet shows the truth: if the black bar is pale or banded while the colour bars are fine, the black channel is the fault, and the driver has been hiding it.

This matters for two reasons. First, composite black costs more: three colour cartridges draining to print black text is an expensive way to print a letter. Second, it masks the fault. A printer that prints acceptable black from mixed colours can go weeks before anyone notices the black cartridge is empty, and by then the colour cartridges are low too. Print the CMYK sheet when black text looks soft or tinted, and the K bar will tell you whether the black channel is doing its own work.
