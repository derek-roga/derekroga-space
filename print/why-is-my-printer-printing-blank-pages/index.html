Why Is My Printer Printing Blank Pages?

Pages come out with nothing on them. The printer moves, the paper feeds, and the page is blank. Here are the causes, in the order to check them, and the fix for each one.

Quick checks first

Before the cause ladder, run through these five checks. They take two minutes and they solve the problem more often than any of the deeper causes.

  1. Check the paper. Is there paper in the tray, loaded the right way up, with the guides snug? A printer that cannot pick up paper properly can feed a blank sheet, or feed two sheets at once so the printed one stays inside.
  2. Check the ink or toner. Open the printer's status screen and look at the levels. An empty cartridge is the commonest cause of blank pages, and it is the easiest to miss when the printer reports levels optimistically.
  3. Check the connection. Is the printer connected, by cable or Wi-Fi, and is it the printer selected in the print dialog? A job sent to the wrong printer, or to a printer that is offline, can sit in the queue while a different printer feeds blank pages.
  4. Check the queue. Open the print queue and clear any stuck or paused jobs. A corrupted job at the front of the queue can block everything behind it, and some printers feed a blank page when they finally clear it.
  5. Check the document. Print something else, a different file, a single line of text. If a different document prints fine, the fault is in the first file, not the printer. A blank page in a PDF, or a page with white text on a white background, prints blank by design.

If the quick checks do not solve it, work down the cause ladder. The causes are ordered most likely first.

The cause ladder

1. Empty or clogged cartridge

A cartridge can be empty while the printer still reports ink remaining, especially a refilled or third-party cartridge. And a cartridge that has not been used for weeks can have dried ink blocking its nozzles, so nothing comes out even though ink is inside. The fix: remove the cartridge, check the level by weight and by the status screen, and look at the nozzle area for dried ink. If the cartridge is genuinely empty, replace it. If it has ink but the nozzles are blocked, run one head-cleaning cycle from the printer's maintenance menu, then print the Quick sheet to check.

2. Dried print heads

On printers with a separate print head, inkjet heads dry out when the printer sits unused. A head that has been idle for a month can be completely blocked. The fix is the same head-cleaning cycle, run once and then re-tested. If two cycles do not clear it, the head may need a manual clean: your printer's manual will say whether the head is removable and how to clean it. Never poke at the nozzles with a pin or a sharp object; the nozzles are finer than a hair and damage easily.

3. Driver fault

A corrupted or outdated driver can send a job the printer cannot interpret, and the printer responds by feeding a blank page. The fix: remove the printer from your computer's printer settings, restart the computer, and add the printer again with a fresh driver downloaded from the manufacturer's site for your exact model. Do not rely on the driver Windows or macOS installs automatically if the problem persists; the manufacturer's driver is the one that matches the printer.

4. Blank-page or separator setting

Some printers and drivers have a setting that inserts a blank page between jobs, or a separator page at the start of each job. If every job prints one good page followed by a blank one, or a blank page before the real output, this setting is the likely cause. Check the printer's settings for "separator page", "blank page" or "job separator" and turn it off.

5. Paper-feed issue

If the printer picks up two sheets at once, it prints on the first and the second comes out blank, or it prints on neither and both come out blank. The fix: fan the paper stack before loading it, do not overfill the tray, and make sure the paper guides are snug against the stack. Damp or humid paper sticks together more, so store paper flat in a dry place and keep the ream wrapped until you load it.

Prove the fix

Once you have applied a fix, print the Quick sheet from TestPrintPage. If it prints with content on it, the blank-page fault is fixed. Then print the Black and White sheet to check the black channel properly: a printer that has just recovered from a clog often prints, but prints faintly or with bands, and the B&W sheet shows that clearly. If the Quick sheet is still blank after the fixes above, work through the ladder once more, and check the driver step carefully: it is the one most often skipped.

When to suspect hardware failure

If the printer feeds paper, the cartridges have ink, the heads have been cleaned, the driver is fresh, and the printer's own self-test page is also blank, the fault is in the hardware. The usual suspects are a failed print head, a failed ink delivery system, or a main-board fault. At that point the honest answer is a repair quote or a replacement, and the cost of the quote should be weighed against the cost of a new printer. One last check before that: print the printer's own self-test page from its panel, with no computer involved. If that page is blank too, the computer and the driver are ruled out, and the fault is inside the printer.
