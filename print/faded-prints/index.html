Faded Prints

Prints come out light, washed out, or patchy. Faded is not one fault, it is three: light overall, patchy, or one colour faded. Each has different causes and different fixes.

What "faded" looks like

Before fixing anything, look at the print and name the pattern. The pattern is the diagnosis.

  • Light overall. Everything on the page is pale, but even. Text is readable but grey instead of black. Photos look washed out. The whole page is uniformly too light.
  • Patchy. Some areas are fine and others are pale. Bands of light and dark across the page, or pale patches in one corner. The unevenness is the clue.
  • One colour faded. Black is fine but a colour is weak, or one colour is fine and the rest are pale. The fault is in one channel, not in the printer as a whole.

Each pattern points at a different cause ladder, so identifying it first saves a lot of wasted cleaning cycles and cartridge changes.

The cause ladder

Low ink or toner

The commonest cause of uniformly light prints. On an inkjet, a cartridge that is nearly empty prints pale before it prints nothing. On a laser printer, low toner gives the same effect, often with pale patches first, then uniform fading. The fix: check the levels on the printer's status screen, and replace the low cartridge. On a laser printer, taking the toner cartridge out and rocking it gently side to side can redistribute the remaining toner and buy a few more prints, but it is a stopgap, not a fix.

Draft or ink-saving mode

This is the commonest "fault" that is not a fault at all. Draft mode, economy mode, toner-save mode and ink-saving mode all print lighter by design, using less ink or toner per page. They are useful for internal drafts and terrible for anything that matters. The fix: open the print dialog, check the quality setting, and set it to Normal or Best for the print you care about. Then check whether the setting is stuck as a default: if every print comes out light, the driver default may have been changed to draft mode at some point and never changed back. Set the default to Normal in the printer's properties.

Clogged nozzles

On an inkjet, nozzles clog when the printer sits unused, and a partly clogged head prints pale or banded output. The fix is one head-cleaning cycle from the printer's maintenance menu, then a reprint. If the print improves but is not clean, run it once more. Beyond two cycles, further cleaning wastes ink without helping, and the cartridge becomes the more likely fault.

Paper type

Paper affects fading more than most people expect. Paper that is too absorbent soaks up ink before it can form a sharp image, giving a pale, soft print. Glossy paper printed with the paper type set to plain gets too little ink for its surface. The fix: set the paper type in the print dialog to match the paper in the tray, and use fresh, dry paper. Paper that has absorbed humidity from the air prints noticeably worse than paper from a sealed ream.

Humidity and storage

Both the printer and the paper are affected by humidity. A printer in a damp room can have condensation on the drum or the head, and paper stored in a damp place absorbs moisture that changes how ink sits on it. The fix: store paper flat, in its wrapper, in a dry room, and keep the printer in a room that is neither damp nor dusty. If the printer has been in a cold garage or a damp spare room, let it warm up to room temperature for an hour before judging its output.

Fix per cause, in order

  1. Check the quality setting. Set it to Normal. If the next print is fine, you are done: it was draft mode.
  2. Check the ink or toner levels. Replace any cartridge that is low. On a laser printer, rock the toner cartridge gently first to see if the fading clears.
  3. Run one head-cleaning cycle. Inkjet only. Then reprint the same page and compare.
  4. Check the paper. Fresh paper from a sealed ream, correct paper type setting, guides snug.
  5. Print the B&W sheet. If the fading persists after the steps above, print the Black and White test page and read the grey ramp. It will show whether the fault is in the black channel or across the whole printer.

Prove the fix

Print the Black and White sheet first. If the solid blacks are solid and the grey ramp shows distinct steps, the mono output is healthy. Then print the Colour sheet to check colour the same way. Print both sheets before the fix and after it if you can: the pair of prints is the evidence that the fix worked, and it shows immediately if a second fault is hiding behind the first. A short monthly maintenance routine keeps the fading from coming back.
