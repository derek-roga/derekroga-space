How to Print a Test Page

The fastest way to check a printer is to print one page and look at it. Here are the routes: the Quick sheet from TestPrintPage, Windows settings, macOS settings, and the printer's own panel.

The fastest way: the Quick sheet

If you want an answer in two minutes, print the Quick sheet from TestPrintPage. It is one page that checks everything at a glance: colour, black, alignment and text sharpness in a single print. If the Quick sheet looks right, your printer is healthy. If something on it looks wrong, the sheet tells you which detailed sheet to print next.

  1. Open TestPrintPage. Go to testprintpage.net and pick the Quick sheet in the paper size that matches the paper in your tray, A4 or US Letter.
  2. Print at 100 percent. In the print dialog, set scaling to 100 percent or "Actual size". Do not use "Fit to page": scaling changes the sheet and the sheet stops measuring what it claims to measure.
  3. Look at the print. Check the colour blocks, the black areas, the fine text and the alignment marks. If all of them look right, the printer is fine. If one of them is wrong, follow the guide for that symptom.

Print at 100 percent. This is the single most common mistake with test pages. "Fit to page" and "Shrink to fit" resize the sheet, move the margins, and make a healthy printer look faulty. Always print test pages at 100 percent, on the paper size the sheet was made for. If your prints keep coming out the wrong size, see A4 vs US Letter.

From Windows settings

Windows has a built-in test page. It is a basic page, mostly text with a small graphic, and it is useful for one question: does the printer respond and print at all? It will not tell you much about colour accuracy or alignment, which is why the Quick sheet is the better first step for diagnosis. But if you cannot reach a website, or you want to confirm the driver is working, the built-in page is the right tool.

  1. Open printer settings. Open Settings, then Bluetooth and devices, then Printers and scanners. In older Windows versions the same screen lives in the Control Panel under Devices and Printers.
  2. Open your printer. Select the printer from the list, then open its properties or printer properties screen. The exact wording varies by Windows version.
  3. Print the test page. Look for the "Print Test Page" button, usually on the first tab of the properties window. Click it, and the printer should produce its built-in test page within a few seconds.

If nothing prints, check the print queue for a stuck job, check the cable or Wi-Fi connection, and make sure the right printer is set as default. A test page that never arrives is itself a diagnosis: the problem is the connection or the driver, not the ink.

From macOS settings

macOS does not have a one-click test page button in the way Windows does. The usual route is to print a document you already have, or to print the Quick sheet from TestPrintPage in your browser, which is the better test anyway. If you want to check the printer through the system itself, the route is through System Settings.

  1. Open printer settings. Open System Settings, then Printers and Scanners, and select your printer.
  2. Check the queue. Open the print queue from the same screen. If jobs are stuck or paused, clear them first: a stuck job blocks everything behind it.
  3. Print something. The practical test on a Mac is to print a real page. The Quick sheet from TestPrintPage is the best choice, because it actually tests colour, black and alignment. A blank document proves only that paper moves.

On some printers, the macOS driver offers a "Print Test Page" option inside the printer's utility software, which is usually installed alongside the driver or downloadable from the manufacturer's site. If your printer has one, it is worth knowing about, but the browser route is faster and the sheet is more informative.

From the printer's own panel

Most printers can print a test or status page from their own control panel, with no computer involved. This is the most useful test when you suspect the computer, the driver, or the connection, because it takes all three out of the picture. If the printer's own test page prints cleanly, the printer is fine and the fault is upstream.

The exact steps vary by brand and model, and this guide does not invent menu paths. The usual places to look are a "Reports", "Tools", "Maintenance" or "Diagnostics" menu on the printer's screen, or a combination of physical buttons on printers without a screen. Your printer's manual, or the manufacturer's support site for your model, has the exact steps. The page it prints is usually called a "configuration page", "status page", "print quality diagnostic page" or "test page", and it typically includes ink or toner levels, which is useful information on its own.

One note on brands: HP, Epson and Canon all offer a self-test page, but the menus differ enough that a single set of steps would be wrong for two readers out of three. The principle is the same everywhere, though: the printer's own page tests the printer alone, and a clean self-test page with bad computer prints points at the driver or the connection.

What to look at first once it prints

Hold the printed sheet at arm's length and check four things, in this order.

  • Is anything there at all? A blank page means ink, heads, or feed. See why is my printer printing blank pages.
  • Are the colours right? Check the colour blocks against what they should be. If a colour is missing or wrong, print the colour test page, or the CMYK test page to isolate the channel.
  • Is the black solid and the text sharp? Grey instead of black, or fuzzy text, points at the black and white test page and the faded prints guide.
  • Are the lines straight and single? Doubled or offset lines mean alignment. See the printer alignment test.

Whatever you find, print the matching sheet and follow its guide. And always print the same sheet again after a fix. The second print is the proof.

Wrong paper size?

If the sheet prints too small, too large, or cut off at the edges, the paper size is probably mismatched: an A4 sheet printed on Letter paper, or the reverse. Set the right size in the print dialog and in the PDF, and print again at 100 percent. The full explanation, with a size table and fix steps, is in A4 vs US Letter.
