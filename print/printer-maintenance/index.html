Printer Maintenance

Most printer problems are preventable. Ten minutes a month, four simple habits, and the printer that works today keeps working.

The monthly 10 minutes

A printer does not need much. It needs to be used, kept clean, and kept supplied. Ten minutes once a month covers all three, and it prevents most of the faults the diagnosis guides on this site are written to fix. The routine below is the whole of it. There is no step six.

  1. Print a test page. Start with the Quick sheet from TestPrintPage. It is the baseline for everything else: if the test page looks right, the printer is healthy and the rest of the routine is a two-minute check. If it does not look right, the fault is visible now, while it is still small, instead of on the day you need to print something important.
  2. Check the ink or toner. Look at the levels on the printer's status screen or in the driver. Replace a cartridge that is nearly empty before it runs dry mid-print, and keep one spare black cartridge on hand if you print regularly. A cartridge that runs dry can let air into an inkjet head, which turns a cheap refill into a cleaning job. On a laser printer, a toner cartridge that is nearly empty often prints pale patches first, so a low reading plus pale output is a clear signal, not a mystery.
  3. Clean the heads, only if the test page says so. On an inkjet, one cleaning cycle from the printer's maintenance menu clears a partly clogged head. Run it when the test page shows banding, missing lines or pale patches, and not otherwise. Cleaning is a repair, not a habit, and the section below explains why running it on a schedule does more harm than good.
  4. Update the driver. Check for a driver update from the printer maker's own site once a month, or let the operating system handle it if it already does. Driver updates fix the quiet faults: prints that come out the wrong size, colour management that drifts after an operating system update, and features that stop working after a major system upgrade. If everything is working and no update is offered, leave it alone. An update is worth doing, not worth chasing.
  5. Wipe the paper path. Open the printer, take out the paper tray, and look for dust, loose paper fibres and stray sheets. Wipe the pickup rollers with a dry, lint-free cloth, and clear any dust from the tray. Dust on the rollers is the commonest cause of paper feeding crooked or feeding two sheets at once, and it takes thirty seconds to clear. Do not use water or cleaning spray inside the printer, and never touch the inside of a laser printer's drum unit.

That is the routine. It is deliberately short, because a routine that takes an hour does not get done, and a routine that does not get done prevents nothing. Ten minutes, once a month, on the same day if you can manage it, is worth more than a deep clean once a year.

Paper matters more than you think

Paper causes more printer problems than ink does, and it gets less attention. Three habits cover it.

Store it flat and dry

Paper absorbs moisture from the air. A ream left open in a damp room will print worse than paper from a sealed pack, with pale, soft output and a tendency to curl and jam. Store paper flat, in its wrapper, in a dry room, and only load what you will use in the next few weeks. Paper that has curled at the edges is telling you it has absorbed moisture. It will jam, and it will print badly, so replace it rather than fighting it.

Load it properly

Fan the stack before loading it, so the sheets separate instead of sticking together, then tap it flat on the desk. Slide the paper guides in until they just touch the stack, snug but not tight. Guides left loose let the paper feed crooked. Guides squeezed tight bend the stack and cause jams. Do not overfill the tray: the fill line is there because an overfilled tray feeds two sheets at once.

Use the right size

The paper size in the tray and the paper size in the print dialog must match, or the print comes out scaled, cropped or centred on the wrong part of the page. If your test pages print at the wrong size, the size setting is the first thing to check, not the printer. The A4 vs US Letter guide covers the sizes, the table, and the fix in full.

When not to clean

Cleaning cycles use ink. Every head-cleaning cycle pushes ink through the nozzles to clear them, and that ink comes out of your cartridges. A cycle run when the heads are already clean is ink thrown away, and a printer that runs a cleaning cycle every time it is switched on will empty its cartridges faster than a printer that is simply left alone. Run a cleaning cycle only when the test page shows a fault that cleaning can fix: banding, missing lines, or pale patches on an inkjet.

Two cycles is the limit. If the print improves after one cycle but is not clean, run it once more. If two cycles have not fixed it, further cleaning will not help either, and the fault is somewhere else: a cartridge that is genuinely empty, a head that has dried out beyond what cleaning can clear, or a problem that is not a clog at all. At that point, stop cleaning and work through the cause ladder on the relevant diagnosis guide instead.

The same restraint applies to the inside of the printer. Do not open a laser printer and clean the drum with a cloth or liquid: the drum surface is delicate, light-sensitive and easily damaged, and a damaged drum prints worse than a dusty one. Do not vacuum toner out of a laser printer with a household vacuum, because toner is a fine powder that passes straight through a normal filter and back into the room. Dust the outside, wipe the rollers, clear the tray, and leave the inside to the printer's own maintenance routines.

The symptoms this prevents

Every diagnosis guide on this site describes a fault that routine maintenance makes less likely. The connection is direct.

  • Blank pages. A monthly test page catches a cartridge running dry or a head drying out before it reaches the point of printing nothing. A printer that is used and checked does not silently run dry.
  • Faded prints. Checking ink levels monthly means a low cartridge is replaced before it prints pale, and checking the quality setting means draft mode is caught before it becomes the default nobody remembers setting.
  • Wrong colours. A monthly colour test page shows a weakening channel while it is still a cleaning job, not after it has become a missing colour. The CMYK sheet isolates the channel, and the routine catches the drift early.
  • Streaks and lines. Clean rollers and a clear paper path prevent the feed faults that cause streaks, and a monthly test page catches a clogged nozzle before it has been printing bands for a month.

Maintenance does not make a printer immortal. Printers wear out, cartridges run dry, and parts fail. What the routine does is move the failures from "sudden, on the worst day" to "visible early, fixable cheaply". That is the whole point of it.

Prove it works

Print the Quick sheet from TestPrintPage before the routine and after it, and keep the two prints side by side. If the after print is cleaner than the before print, the routine did its job. If both prints are clean, the printer is healthy and the routine took two minutes. Either way, you have evidence instead of a guess, and the next time something goes wrong, you will know when it last worked.
