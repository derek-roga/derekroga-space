Colour Test Page

A colour test page checks what your printer does with colour: blocks, gradients and tone bars that show a missing channel, a clogged head, or a colour that has drifted.

What a colour test page checks

A colour test page is a printed sheet covered in colour: solid blocks, smooth gradients, and tone bars that step from light to dark. Each element tests a different part of colour printing. The solid blocks show whether each colour prints at all. The gradients show whether colours blend smoothly or break into bands. The tone bars show whether light shades stay light and dark shades stay dark, or whether everything collapses toward the middle.

You need one when colours look wrong, when a photo prints dull or tinted, when one colour seems to be missing entirely, or when you simply want to know the printer is healthy before printing something that matters. It is also the right sheet after any fix that touches colour: a head cleaning, a cartridge change, or a driver update. Print the sheet before the fix and after it, and the two prints tell you whether the fix worked.

Note for US readers: this page uses the British spelling "colour" throughout. A colour test page and a color test page are the same thing, and search engines treat them that way. The sheet does not care how you spell it.

How to print one

Print the Colour sheet from TestPrintPage, free, no sign-up. Pick the paper size that matches the paper in your tray, A4 or US Letter, and print at 100 percent with no scaling. If you are not sure about the print dialog settings, the how to print a test page guide walks through the steps on Windows, Mac, and from the printer itself.

Use plain white paper, not coloured or recycled stock, and let the print dry for a minute before judging it. Inkjet prints look darker when wet, and a hasty reading can send you chasing a fault that is not there.

How to read it

Work through the sheet element by element. Each one has a job.

Colour blocks

The solid blocks of red, green, blue, yellow, cyan and magenta are the first thing to check. Each block should be one flat colour, edge to edge, with no white streaks, no pale patches, and no banding. A block that is missing entirely means its channel is not printing. A block with white lines through it means clogged nozzles in that channel. A block that is the wrong colour, green where blue should be, or a muddy brown where red should be, means the channels are mixing or the wrong ink is reaching the paper.

Gradients

The gradient bars should blend smoothly from one colour to another, or from full colour to white. If a gradient breaks into visible steps or bands, the printer is struggling with fine gradations. That can be a clogged head, a low cartridge, or a driver setting that is reducing quality. Banding that appears in every gradient, in every colour, points at the print head or the paper feed. Banding in one colour only points at that colour's channel.

Tone bars and skin tones

Tone bars step from light to dark in even increments. Each step should be visibly different from the next. If the light steps all look white, the printer is losing highlight detail. If the dark steps all look black, it is losing shadow detail. Skin-tone bars, where the sheet has them, are the most sensitive test of all: human eyes are extremely good at spotting a wrong skin tone, so a cast that is invisible in a blue block will show up immediately in a face.

If a colour is missing

When one colour does not print at all, the next step is to find out whether the channel is empty or just blocked. Print the CMYK test page, which isolates cyan, magenta, yellow and black into separate bars. If the missing colour's bar is blank on the CMYK sheet too, the cartridge may be empty or the head may be clogged. Run one head-cleaning cycle from your printer's maintenance menu, then print the CMYK sheet again. One cleaning cycle is usually enough; running it five times in a row wastes ink and rarely helps more than the first two.

If the colour returns after cleaning, the head was clogged, often because the printer sat unused for a while. If it does not return, check the cartridge level and, if the cartridge is a refill or a third-party one, consider that the cartridge itself may be the fault. A cartridge that reports as full but prints nothing is a known failure mode of cheap refills.

If colours look wrong

When colours print but look wrong, a tint over everything, a cast that shifts every colour in one direction, or colours that look muddy, the fault is usually colour management rather than ink. Check the paper type setting in the print dialog first: printing a photo on plain paper with the paper type set to glossy, or the reverse, changes how the driver lays down ink. Then check whether the driver is managing colour or the application is. If both try to manage it at once, colours double-correct and come out wrong. The full cause ladder is in the printer printing wrong colours guide.

Colour sheet checklist

  • All colour blocks present, flat, and the right colour
  • Gradients smooth, no visible bands or steps
  • Tone bars show distinct steps from light to dark
  • No white streaks or pale patches inside any block
  • Skin tones look natural, with no green, pink or orange cast

If every item passes, your colour output is healthy. If one fails, the guides above will take you to the fix.
