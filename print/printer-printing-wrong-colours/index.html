Printer Printing Wrong Colours

Colours come out tinted, shifted, or missing. The three patterns, colour cast, missing colour, and muddy colours, each point at different causes, and the CMYK sheet is the fastest way to tell them apart.

The three patterns

Name the pattern first. It decides everything that follows.

Colour cast

Everything is tinted in one direction: all the whites look pink, or all the photos look green, or everything has a blue chill. The colours are present but shifted. A cast usually means colour management, not ink: the driver or the application is correcting colour in a way that does not match the paper or the printer.

Missing colour

One colour is gone entirely. Reds print yellow because the magenta is missing. Greens print blue because the yellow is missing. Skin tones go grey or green. A missing colour means one ink channel is not printing, and the CMYK sheet will name it in one print.

Swapped or muddy colours

Colours are present but wrong in a confused way: browns where reds should be, muddy greens, colours that look dirty. This usually means the channels are mixing, because a head is clogged and ink is cross-contaminating, or because a cartridge is leaking into a neighbouring channel. It can also mean the wrong paper profile is telling the driver the paper is something it is not.

Isolate with the CMYK sheet

The central diagnostic step is the CMYK test page. Print it from TestPrintPage before changing anything. It prints each ink channel on its own: cyan, magenta, yellow and black, each in a separate bar.

  • One bar is blank or banded. That channel is the fault. Clean the head once, reprint, and if the bar does not recover, check that cartridge.
  • All four bars are clean. The ink and the heads are fine. The fault is colour management, the paper type setting, or the paper profile. Skip the cleaning cycles and go to the colour-management causes below.
  • All four bars are bad. A fault shared by every channel: the head, the feed, or the paper. Clean the head, check the paper, and check the feed.

This one sheet saves the most common mistake in colour diagnosis: replacing a cartridge when the fault was a setting, or changing settings for an hour when the fault was an empty cartridge.

The cause ladder

Empty channel

A cartridge that is empty, or a channel that has run dry, removes one colour from every mixture. The fix is a new cartridge for that channel. Check the level first, and remember that a cartridge can report ink remaining while printing nothing, especially a refilled or third-party cartridge.

Clogged head

A partly clogged head in one channel gives banded or pale output in that channel, which shifts every colour that uses it. The fix is one head-cleaning cycle, then a reprint of the CMYK sheet. Two cycles at most; beyond that, the cartridge is the more likely fault.

Wrong paper profile

The paper type setting in the print dialog tells the driver how much ink to lay down and how to correct colour for that paper. Printing a photo on plain paper with the setting on glossy, or the reverse, changes the colours visibly. The fix: set the paper type to match the paper in the tray, and if your driver offers paper profiles, use the one for your exact paper.

Driver colour management

Colour can be managed by the application or by the driver, but not by both at once. If both try to correct it, colours double-correct and come out wrong, usually oversaturated or shifted. The fix: pick one. In most cases, let the printer manage colour and turn off colour management in the application, or the reverse, but never both. This setting lives in the print dialog's colour management section, and its exact wording varies by application and driver.

Cheap or refilled cartridge

Third-party and refilled cartridges can have ink that is a slightly different shade from the manufacturer's, which shifts every colour that uses that channel. A refilled cartridge can also have a damaged chip that misreports its level. The fix: if the colours went wrong right after a cartridge change, and the CMYK sheet shows that channel printing but the wrong shade, the cartridge is the prime suspect. A manufacturer cartridge, or a reputable third-party one, is the test.

Fix per cause, in order

  1. Print the CMYK sheet. Name the failing channel, or rule out the ink entirely.
  2. If one channel is failing: clean the head once, reprint, and replace the cartridge if the bar does not recover.
  3. If all channels are clean: check the paper type setting, then check that only one colour manager is active.
  4. If the fault followed a cartridge change: suspect the cartridge, and test with a different one.
  5. Prove the fix: print the Colour sheet and compare it with the print from before the fix.
